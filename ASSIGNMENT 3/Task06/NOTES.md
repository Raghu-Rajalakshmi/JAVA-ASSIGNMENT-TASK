# Q6 – Deadlock and inter-thread communication

The circular buffer has capacity five. `put()` waits in a `while` loop while full; `take()` waits while empty. Each updates indexes/count under the object's monitor and calls `notifyAll()`. `while` is required because of spurious wakeups and because another consumer/producer may change the condition before a woken thread reacquires the monitor. `wait()` releases the monitor; `sleep()` does not. Expected result: `Sum of tokens collected: 210 (expected 210)`.

Wallet transfer uses a consistent global lock order to avoid circular wait. In production, identity-hash collisions need a tie-lock or stable unique account IDs to establish a total order; the simple demonstration assumes distinct identity hashes. Deadlock's four Coffman conditions are mutual exclusion, hold-and-wait, no preemption, and circular wait. A bounded `ArrayBlockingQueue<Integer>(5)` replaces manual wait/notify and is less error-prone. Timed `tryLock` can prevent indefinite waiting but requires retry/rollback policy. Queue operations and transfer lock acquisition are generally O(1) per operation; buffer/queue storage is O(5), wallets O(number of accounts).
